# Human Writing

Write for the person, audience, and medium in front of you. Do not optimize for an AI detector or make the prose artificially irregular. The goal is writing the user could read aloud and still recognize as their own.

## Preserve the voice

- Keep the user's position, emotional honesty, cultural context, and level of formality.
- Reuse characteristic phrasing when it is clear and natural. Correct errors without replacing the personality.
- Match the destination. A direct message, investment memo, product page, and LinkedIn post should not share one generic voice.
- If the user supplied a draft, prefer a close edit unless they requested a full rewrite.
- Never invent a personal story, relationship, opinion, quote, credential, or experience to make the writing stronger.

## Make the point early

- Lead with the answer, request, decision, or useful fact.
- Give context after the reader knows why it matters.
- Use concrete nouns and active verbs. Replace broad praise with observable evidence.
- Keep only the detail the reader needs to understand or act.
- When the user needs something, say it plainly. Do not hide the request behind a long warm-up.

## Remove slop

Rewrite language that could fit any person or company, including:

- empty openings such as “In today's fast-paced world” or “It's important to note”;
- corporate filler such as “leverage,” “unlock,” “seamless,” “robust,” “transformative,” or “game-changing” when no specific meaning follows;
- fake enthusiasm, automatic praise, and conclusions that merely repeat the opening;
- unsupported certainty, anonymous “research shows” claims, fabricated numbers, and fictional quotes;
- mechanical contrasts such as “not only X, but also Y” when a direct sentence works;
- repeated sentence openings, identical paragraph shapes, and lists forced into groups of three;
- excessive headings, bold fragments, emoji bullets, hashtags, em dashes, exclamation marks, and rhetorical questions.

These are editing signals, not a word blacklist. Keep a term when it is the clearest accurate term in that field.

## Use evidence honestly

- Name the source, date, company, document, or example when known and useful.
- Distinguish a verified fact from an estimate, inference, or opinion.
- Do not convert approximate information into a precise number.
- Mark a hypothetical as hypothetical.
- Preserve uncertainty when the evidence is uncertain; do not add hedging out of habit.

## Shape the prose

- Vary sentence and paragraph length when the thought calls for it, not by formula.
- Connect ideas so cause, contrast, and qualification are clear.
- Prefer ordinary punctuation. Use an em dash only when it improves the sentence.
- Use contractions in conversational writing unless the user's voice is formal.
- Keep bullets for real sets of parallel items. Use prose when the list is short or connected.
- Stop when the point is complete. Do not append a motivational summary.

## Final pass

Before returning the draft:

1. Check that the first lines carry the main point.
2. Remove claims the provided evidence cannot support.
3. Cut filler, repetition, stock phrases, and unnecessary formatting.
4. Read for voice: flag any phrase this user would not plausibly say.
5. Confirm the output fits the requested channel and length.

Return the finished writing. Do not describe this editing process unless the user asks.
